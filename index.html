Golf by Ear: Motion Edition

An audio golf game for blind and visually impaired players, and anyone else. In this Motion Edition you turn your body to face the flag, swing the phone like a club, putt with it like a pendulum, and walk to your ball. Tap swing and every button still work. Sounds are made by your phone as you play; there are no recordings.

    How to play

    Full swing, tap style

    Press and hold the pad or the Swing button. A tone rises as you take the club back. About one second is a full swing, and a click tells you when you reach it. Let go when the tone is high enough. Shorter holds give shorter shots. Then you hear three beats: tick, tick, TOCK. Tap on the TOCK. Tap early and the ball curves left; tap late and it curves right. Left-handed players get the mirror image. If you miss the beats entirely, no stroke is counted.

    With VoiceOver you can double tap and hold the pad. Or double tap the Swing button once to start the backswing, double tap again to stop it, then double tap on the TOCK.

    Motion: getting started

    Press Turn on motion controls and choose Allow when the phone asks. The way you face then is 12 o'clock. Try the motion training range first. Hold on tight, use a wrist strap if you have one, and keep clear of people, pets and furniture.

    Motion: aiming by turning

    A bell rings from the flag. Turn your body, with the phone held in front of you, until the bell sits in the middle. When you are on line you hear a little rising chime and then a steady soft tone, and the bell rings more slowly. The closer you are, the faster the bell rings. If the bell sounds muffled, the flag is behind you. Your aim follows the way you face until you start your swing; then it is locked. Where is the flag, or key F, tells you the clock bearing, like "The flag is at 2 o'clock". With aim assist on, nearly on the flag counts as dead on. Each hole points a different way in your room, so you will turn as you go round; a shot that goes right leaves the flag a little to your left. On the green, turning moves the putt gently: about 3 degrees of turning for 1 degree of putt. With putting hints on, a soft double tick tells you when you face the suggested line for the break. The break rumble comes from the downhill side, wherever you face.

    Motion: swinging

    Hold the phone upright in both hands like a golf grip, screen towards you. Keep still until you hear two quick beeps. Take the phone back slowly: a tone rises as you go, and a click tells you that you have reached a full swing. Then swing through with a sharp strike. The length of your backswing sets the power, and a firm strike adds a little. The time from the top to the strike sets the curve: about a quarter of a second is straight, a rushed downswing pulls the ball left, and a slow one pushes it right. Turning your body never starts a swing. If no strike comes, no stroke is counted. To change club, flick your wrist sharply, twisting the phone one way for the next club and the other way to go back, or use Next club. You can still use tap swing on the pad or the Swing button at any time.

    Motion: putting

    With the putter, let the phone hang in both hands like a putter. Keep still for the two beeps, swing it back gently while the tone rises, then forward through where you started. The longer the backstroke, the further the ball rolls. Read the green plays the pace tone for the suggested putt. Two taps on the pad still work as before.

    Motion: walking the course

    After a shot you can walk a few steps to your ball. You hear your footsteps on grass or sand, and the sounds of the course pass by. When you arrive you are told your lie and distance. Jab the phone, press Skip the walk, or choose Off in Motion controls to skip it. When a hole is finished, a jab of the phone goes on to the next player or hole.

    Motion: recentring

    Press Recentre, key C, or put the phone face down for a second and pick it up: the way you face becomes straight ahead again. Do it if the flag seems to drift.

    Putting

    Choose the putter. Tap once to start the stroke, and tap again to strike. A short gap is a firm putt; a long gap is a gentle one. Soft clicks count quarter seconds between your taps, with a higher click every second. About a tenth of a second hits it a very long way; one second is about twenty feet on a flat green; one and a half seconds is about four feet. Leave it longer than two seconds and the putt is cancelled without a stroke.

    On the green, a low rumble comes from the side the ball will drift towards, and it gets louder on steeper slopes. Read the green tells you the distance, slope and break, and with putting hints on, the aim and gap to try.

    Sounds to listen for

    • A faint steady tone is the wind: higher means into the wind, lower means it is helping, and it sits on the side the wind comes from. On the green the same tone tells you the grain: higher means into the grain, which is slower.
    • The swish of the ball moves to the left or right, to where it will land.
    • Landing: a thump on the fairway, a rustle in the rough, a gravelly scrape in sand, a splash in water, a soft thud on the green, a clack on a road.
    • Rolling: a ticking roll that slows down as the ball slows. In the hole: a metal rattle and a clunk. A lip-out: a ping, a whirl and two soft buzzes.

    Rules

    Water costs one penalty stroke, and you drop beside it, no nearer the hole. Deep pot bunkers, like Hell Bunker and the Road Hole Bunker, only let you out with the wedge. After ten strokes on a hole you pick up and score ten. Aim left and Aim right move your aim in steps of five yards, or three inches on the green, and reset after every shot.

    Vibration

    Vibration works only where the browser allows it, such as Chrome on Android. Safari on iPhone does not let web pages vibrate, so every vibration also has a sound.

    Keyboard keys

    • Space: hold to swing, or tap twice to putt. Escape: cancel the swing.
    • 1, 2, 3, 4: driver, iron, wedge, putter.
    • Left and right arrows: aim.
    • I: where am I. W: wind. H: hazards. G: read the green. S: score. R: repeat. N: next hole when a hole is finished.
    • F or B: where is the flag. X: next club. Z: previous club. M: motion controls on or off. C: recentre. K: skip the walk. T: motion training range.

    Playing together

    Choose up to four players. Each player plays the whole hole in turn, and the game says whose turn it is. Pass the phone, or take turns at the keyboard. Your round is saved after every shot; if the page closes, use Resume round.

    The courses

    Meadow Park is invented: a friendly parkland course for learning.

    Fairways of History has three holes based on the Old Course at St Andrews: the first, Burn, with the Swilcan Burn; the fourteenth, Long, with the Beardies and Hell Bunker; and the seventeenth, the Road Hole, with its bunker, road and wall. Then three holes in the style of Pinehurst Number 2, with crowned greens and sandy waste areas. The real holes are simplified; lengths and shapes are approximate.

    The last three holes are imagined. There has never been a golf course at El Alamein. These holes are set in the desert near the railway halt and the sea, as a way to remember the history of 1942. You hear wind, sand, the sea and, now and then, a distant steam train. There are no battle sounds.

    Sounds, credits and licence

    Every sound is synthesised in your browser with the Web Audio API. No recorded sounds are used, so there are no sound credits to give. Speech uses your device's own voices.
